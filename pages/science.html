Core Mechanisms

The Three Drivers of
Hypertrophy

Every exercise, rep range, and rest period in RepForge is chosen to optimize these three mechanisms — simultaneously, not in isolation.

01
⚙️

Mechanical Tension

The primary driver of muscle growth. Created when a muscle actively resists force across its full range of motion — especially at long muscle lengths. RepForge prioritizes exercises with high tension at full stretch, like Romanian deadlifts over conventional, and incline curls over standing curls.

Key research Schoenfeld et al. (2010), McMahon et al. (2014), Oranchuk et al. (2019)
02
🔥

Metabolic Stress

The "pump" — metabolite accumulation (lactate, H+, inorganic phosphate) during higher-rep, shorter-rest training. RepForge uses metabolic stress as a secondary stimulus: programmed into higher-rep finisher sets and drop sets rather than competing with mechanical tension for primary volume.

Key research Goto et al. (2004), Schoenfeld (2013), Pearson & Hussain (2015)
03
🧬

Muscle Damage

Eccentric-heavy loading causes ultrastructural disruption that triggers satellite cell activation and satellite-mediated hypertrophy. RepForge controls muscle damage carefully — too much causes excessive soreness without added growth. Each muscle group's damage tolerance is tracked across your training history.

Key research Proske & Morgan (2001), Damas et al. (2016), Hyldahl & Hubal (2014)
Volume Landmarks

MEV, MAV, MRV —
Your Personal Thresholds

Volume is not one-size-fits-all. RepForge calculates three critical thresholds for each of your muscle groups based on your training age, genetics proxies, and logged performance:

MEV
Minimum Effective Volume

The fewest sets per week needed to maintain or begin growing a muscle. Your starting point for a new mesocycle after a deload.

MAV
Maximum Adaptive Volume

The sweet spot. The volume range where you grow the fastest with the best recovery. RepForge targets this for most of each mesocycle.

MRV
Maximum Recoverable Volume

The ceiling. Going above this causes fatigue accumulation faster than adaptation. RepForge never exceeds your MRV — and auto-deloads when you approach it.

Exercise Selection

The SFR Framework:
Not All Exercises Are Equal

Stimulus-to-Fatigue Ratio quantifies how much muscle stimulus an exercise produces relative to the systemic fatigue it generates. Higher SFR = more growth, faster recovery.

Exercise Target Muscle SFR Score Why RepForge Rates It
Cable Lateral Raise Side Delts 9.2 Constant tension across ROM, minimal systemic fatigue, easy load management
Romanian Deadlift Hamstrings 9.4 Max tension at stretched position, high hamstring stimulus per systemic cost unit
Incline DB Curl Biceps 9.3 Forces long head stretch, removes cheating, peak tension where it matters most
Barbell Squat Quads / Glutes 7.1 High stimulus but also high systemic fatigue — great for strength, imperfect for pure hypertrophy
Barbell Deadlift Posterior Chain 6.8 Enormous systemic cost — RepForge treats it as a strength movement, not a primary hypertrophy tool
Behind-the-Neck Press Shoulders 4.2 High shoulder impingement risk raises injury-adjusted fatigue cost above most alternatives
